Clutter definition (the one the output is judged by; it extends the list above). Clutter is every boilerplate block inside or beside the main column, plus the global header/navigation and the footer: site navigation; related and recommended items, including product suggestions and accessory lists; share and social buttons; newsletter and subscribe prompts; ads and promotions; cookie notices; footers; author boilerplate, meaning republishing or source notes, 'help improve this page' or feedback boxes, contribution prompts, page update histories and 'last modified' notes, but NOT the byline and the article's own date, which are content; and widgets, meaning interface controls and their status text such as image-gallery controls, save or bookmark prompts, and pickers. Neutral (keep or drop freely): breadcrumbs, in-page tables of contents, tags. Decide by what a block is from your own reading: a block is clutter even when its text differs between pages (repeat=1/2) and even when no suspicion list flags it. The preview's keptBoilerplate cues are hints, not the only evidence. Do not retain an obvious site widget merely by labelling it other. Preserve actual article references, acknowledgements, explanations, figures, tables and code even when their text repeats across pages; repeat alone is not evidence that body prose is noise. For a genuinely title-only page, keep its actual title without inventing a body.
